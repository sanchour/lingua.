What Lingua is

Lingua is a place to live in a language for a while. You read in it, listen to it, watch it, talk and write in it, and every word you meet is kept, so it comes back just before you would have forgotten it.

It is not a game and it is not a school. It is closer to a study at home: a desk by the window, a shelf of books, a pot of tea going cold because the chapter got good. That is why its design is called the Cozy Scriptorium.

Six habits of a Lingua page

Six habits, more than rules. A page that has all six usually needs nothing else.

I.The language comes first

On every page, the language is the biggest, best-set thing there is. A sentence in German gets the serif, the space and the light; the buttons around it step back. If a page has nothing in the language on it, ask whether it should.

Set inTitles in Literata. Reading text in the learner’s own reading font, one of six.

II.Quiet until asked

Show the one thing that matters and keep the rest a click away: details in a drawer, numbers on hover, options under More. Control is never taken away, only folded. Whoever wants every setting finds it; whoever doesn’t never has to see it.

ButNothing important lives only on hover. Whatever a hover shows, a click or a key shows too.

III.One word, everywhere

A word saved while reading is the same word in Thesaurus, in Ludus, in a talk in Sermo and in Ratio’s counts. Design as if each word were a small thing the learner carries from room to room, and let it remember where it was found.

Where words liveThe word memory and the dictionary, Thesaurus › Dictionary (4.0-2).

IV.Honest, never hopeful

Say only what is true and built. No forecasts Lingua can’t keep, no scores that look precise and aren’t, no features promised before they exist. When Lingua doesn’t know, it says so plainly, or says nothing at all.

Taken out2026-10-26: the first start’s “B1 in about 7 months” and its pace verdicts. Not accurate enough to say.

V.The learner holds the pen

Whatever Lingua decides for you can be undone: a word marked known can be unmarked, a level changed, a card edited. Nothing large happens behind the learner’s back. Saving a word is not the same as scheduling it, and one click never quietly grows a deck of fifty.

BorrowedFrom the Lingyu blueprint (2026-10-25): “saving is not scheduling”; anything generated is editable and can be dismissed.

VI.Wide and calm

Use the width: columns, side panels, sticky rails, a list beside its detail instead of a page after it. Every page fits a 1366 × 768 laptop with no little scroll bar sneaking in. The reader and long lists are the only things that scroll, and they do it on purpose.

SizesCheck at 1280, 1440 and 1366 × 768. Shortcuts: Alt 1–8 for the rooms; the rest in the Shortcuts dialog.

If you only remember one: the language first, everything else quiet.

The voice

Lingua talks like a friend who reads a lot: informal, warm, a little dry. It likes to make you smile, but never at the cost of being clear.

One smile per page. A funny subtitle is a gift; three of them are a stand-up set. The joke goes where nobody has to read it to act: a subtitle, a quiet line under a field. Never inside an instruction.

The user’s outlineFor the first start: “minimal, informal, bookish, building up the excitement, with funny subtitles” (5.1-2b).

Titles ask or name. “What language will it be?” “So, how far along are you?” “Choose your study light.” Short enough to take in at a glance, and a real question when there is one.

Buttons say what happens. “Continue with Deutsch” says more than “Next”. One main button per page.

Plain where it matters. Money, privacy, errors and deleting get the plainest words in the app. “Could not save your choices. Please try again.”

Real linesFrom Onboarding.tsx: the error above, and the welcome toast, “Welcome to Lingua, {name}. Happy reading!”

Small manners. British spelling, as in colour and favourite. Curly quotes, and the language’s own quotation marks when its touches are on. No exclamation marks in the interface; the welcome toast may keep its one.

Chapter

Getting the language right

A learner believes every sentence Lingua shows them. A language app is only as good as its language.

Name the source. Every grammar rule is checked against a named source, listed under it, as Grammatica already does. Data keeps its origin: Wiktionary, Tatoeba, Lingua Libre, each with its licence.

Already doneThe commonest words’ meanings hand-checked in Spanish, French, German and Russian (5.1-3a).

A native reader before Core. Before a language becomes Core, and before the interface is translated into it (Lingua 6-19), someone who speaks it natively reads the course, the grammar and the interface. Pay them or thank them by name on the credits page.

Say which variety. Castilian or Mexican Spanish, European or Brazilian Portuguese: the flag’s accent decides the voice, and the words say which they are.

Generated text is a draft. It is labelled, checked before it is shown (Lingua 6-6) and can be reported.

Reports become fixes. A wrong meaning reported by a learner goes on a list, is fixed at the next pack build, and the learner is thanked.

Test sentencesA fixed handful per language (forms, right to left, Japanese without spaces) read in the reader before each release.

Icons: the Lingua hand

An icon in Lingua is a small drawing, not a pictogram. It should look as if someone had sketched it with a fine pen in the margin of a book: one line, a little imperfect, and one small thing in terracotta.

The 48 grid: a live area of 40, the circle, the square, the portrait and the landscape that every icon is fitted to.

  1. The field is 48 × 48, with 4 units of margin all round. Big shapes touch the keylines; small ones centre in them.
  2. One line. Stroke 2.6 on the 48 field: 1.3 px at 24 px, a little under 1 px at 16, 1.7 at 32. Nothing finer than 3 units apart, or it clogs.
  3. Round caps and joins, always; no sharp mitres, no square ends.
  4. Drawn, not ruled. Curves lean by half a unit; a circle is a little egg; lines overshoot a corner by a unit or leave it open.
  5. One idea. A bookmark is a bookmark, not a bookmark plus a star.
  6. One terracotta detail: the part that carries the meaning, under a quarter of the line, never filled except for a dot.

Why these, and not a standard set

A standard set is clean and everywhere; Lingua is a book. The pages already draw a little (the books, the sprig), and icons in the same hand make buttons and empty pages feel like one object. The 33 here are the start of that set.

At night, and how to draw one

At night the line turns soft ivory, terracotta turns amber, sage turns light sage, and a paper fill turns Mocha: the same drawing in a lower light.

How to draw one

  1. Sketch it on paper with a fine pen, three times, and pick the one that is least careful.
  2. Trace it in a vector editor on the 48 field, with a 2.6 stroke at 48 (1.3 at 24), round caps, round joins.
  3. Keep few nodes, twelve at most; let curves do the work.
  4. Pick the one detail that carries the meaning and make only that terracotta.
  5. Test at 16, 24 and 48, in Paper and Nocturne, beside two others from the set.
  6. Name it for its meaning (bookmark: a saved word), and write the meaning beside it.

Before an icon is added

  • It means one thing, and that thing has no icon yet.
  • It reads at 16 px in Paper and in Nocturne, and beside two others from the set.
  • It has an accessible name (its meaning) and, if it stands alone, a tooltip.
  • It is saved as name.svg in docs/handbook/icons, with the line in currentColor.
  • This chapter’s meaning table has a row for it.

The language of the cues

  • Up means well, down means miss, and more notes mean more: one rising pair, a run, an arpeggio. A learner should be able to tell right from wrong with eyes closed.
  • A miss is soft, low and short, never a buzzer. Nothing in Lingua should make a learner flinch.
  • The reader’s buzz: on Android, holding a word for 430 ms gives an 8 ms tap as the drawer opens. That is the only vibration outside a lesson.

Making a new cue

  1. Ask whether it is needed. If the screen already says it, a sound is a second voice.
  2. Build it from the existing family: sine or triangle notes, a few of them, a final note under 0.5 s.
  3. Keep it under the music: peak gain 0.12 at the most.
  4. Give it a vibration only if it matters, shorter than 100 ms in all.
  5. Write it into the table here, with what it means, and test it with the sounds off.
Do
  • Make the off switch one click, in the place the sounds are heard.
  • Play cues after the visible result, not before.
Don’t
  • Play a sound that starts by itself, ever.
  • Make a haptic the only signal: most devices have none.

Designing a new screen

A recipe that works every time. Follow it in order; each step takes minutes, and skipping one is where most mistakes begin.

  1. Write the sentence. “This page is for ___.” One verb; if you need “and”, it is two pages, or a tab.
  2. Pick the shape from the six patterns: dashboard, list with a rail, stage, reader, table, drawer.
  3. Choose the one thing, the single action the page leads to, and where it goes. Everything else is a ghost or a link.
  4. List what to show first and what to keep quiet, as in the room cards: what is on the page at once, and what waits behind a tab, a hover or More.
  5. Draw it in boxes at 1366 × 768 with real content and the longest word; then at 1440 and 1280.
  6. Build with the primitives and the tokens; add nothing new unless you have asked for it in the primitives.

From code to a store, in nine steps

  1. Choose one channel and write down why in docs/DECISIONS.md. One at a time: each has its own account, its own rules and its own reviews.
  2. Open the account. Yours, in your name or your business’s; a session never creates one. Identity checks take days (an Apple enrolment as a company can take two weeks).
  3. Freeze a version. Stores want a plain number: 1.0.0, not 0.5.3-alpha.1. Until you say 1.0.0, use a beta channel (TestFlight, Play testing, a Store flight).
  4. Build the package (a session). A packaging/ folder, away from src/, that points at the live site and keeps the service worker for offline.
  5. Sign it. The keys and certificates are yours, in your password manager, never in the repository or in a chat. The Microsoft Store signs for you.
  6. Test it in the store’s own channel, in a clean user account with no dev profile: first start, offline start, an update, uninstall and reinstall.
  7. Prepare the listing (two pages on): icon, screenshots, words, the privacy answers.
  8. Submit and wait. Usually a day or two, sometimes hours, sometimes a week for a new account. Answer a rejection the same day, in plain words.
  9. Release in stages, watch the reviews and the crash reports, and keep the update path warm.

Keeping learners’ data safe

Everything a learner has done lives in their browser. Losing it would be the worst bug Lingua could have.

The database changes only by a new version with its migration, never by editing an old one; each is tried on a copy of real data first.

NowDexie version 17, src/data/db.ts.

Backups are files the learner makes and keeps. Restoring merges or replaces; the newest change wins row by row, deletions travel along, and keys stay out unless asked.

Wheredata/backup.ts, the merge in core/backup/merge.ts, both tested.

Devices sync directly once paired, with no server between them.

The browser is asked to keep it: persistent storage is requested after the first start, so the data isn’t cleared to save space.

Deleting is said plainly: removing a language names everything that goes with it; Reset says what it deletes; both ask first.

Never a migration that deletes without a backup step; never learning data sent anywhere to be stored.

Before a data change shipsMake a backup, change, restore it, merge it: by hand once, and in the tests.
Chapter

When something breaks

Calmly, in this order: protect the learners’ data, stop the harm, tell people, then fix it properly.

How bad is it?

  1. Data lost or at risk. Everything else waits.
  2. Can’t study: the app won’t open, a room crashes.
  3. Wrong language: a wrong meaning, a wrong rule.
  4. Money: a payment or a licence that doesn’t work.
  5. Looks wrong.

What to do

  1. Revert the push that broke it (git revert, a version, push). Installed apps take the fixed version at their next quiet moment.
  2. If data is at risk, ask learners to make a backup first: a line on the website and in a letter.
  3. Find the cause with Settings › Lab (its reports carry the version) and the tests.
  4. Fix, check, push. Write what happened and why in DECISIONS.md, and a line in the changelog.
  5. Thank the people who wrote in, by name.

NoteA revert is a push like any other: it gets a version.

Chapter

Choosing what’s next

There will always be more to build than time to build it. Six questions, in order, settle most choices.

  1. Does it protect what learners already have: their data, their trust?
  2. Does it stop someone from studying?
  3. Did several learners ask for it, in their own words?
  4. Does it carry one word further: the same word in more places?
  5. What is the smallest version still worth having?
  6. Could it be described honestly on the website the day it ships?

Keep four short lists in the roadmap: now, next, later, and not. “Not” matters most: it is where good ideas go so they stop asking.
