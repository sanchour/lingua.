This book is for whoever designs Lingua next: you, a friend, or a Claude on another account. It says what Lingua is like, so that every page can become a little more like it.

It lives in the repository at docs/handbook. Print it, scribble in it, and change it when Lingua changes.

Before you begin

How to use this book

Read Part One once. It is short, and everything else follows from it.

When you sit down to design a page, open its chapter in Part Three. Every room fits on one page: what the room is for, what it shows first, what it keeps quiet, the one thing to do there, and three questions to ask yourself.

Part One

The spirit

What Lingua is, how it behaves, and how it talks.

Chapter 1

What Lingua is

Lingua is a place to live in a language for a while. You read in it, listen to it, watch it, talk and write in it, and every word you meet is kept, so it comes back just before you would have forgotten it.

It is not a game and it is not a school. It is closer to a study at home: a desk by the window, a shelf of books, a pot of tea going cold because the chapter got good. That is why its design is called the Cozy Scriptorium.

The tokenssrc/design/tokens.css, “Cozy Scriptorium design tokens”.

The rooms have Latin names, because a scriptorium would have used them, and because it is a little bit fun. Each room keeps one kind of work:

Ordersrc/app/sections.ts. Alt 1 to 8 opens them in this order. Ratio and Settings sit apart at the bottom of the sidebar.

Lingua is made for the desktop. It uses a wide window the way you use a desk: things side by side, nothing stacked that could sit next to each other.

Chapter 2

Six habits of a Lingua page

Six habits, more than rules. A page that has all six usually needs nothing else.

I.The language comes first

On every page, the language is the biggest, best-set thing there is. A sentence in German gets the serif, the space and the light; the buttons around it step back. If a page has nothing in the language on it, ask whether it should.

Set inTitles in Literata. Reading text in the learner’s own reading font, one of six.

II.Quiet until asked

Show the one thing that matters and keep the rest a click away: details in a drawer, numbers on hover, options under More. Control is never taken away, only folded. Whoever wants every setting finds it; whoever doesn’t never has to see it.

ButNothing important lives only on hover. Whatever a hover shows, a click or a key shows too.

III.One word, everywhere

A word saved while reading is the same word in Thesaurus, in Ludus, in a talk in Sermo and in Ratio’s counts. Design as if each word were a small thing the learner carries from room to room, and let it remember where it was found.

Where words liveThe word memory and the dictionary, Thesaurus › Dictionary (4.0-2).

IV.Honest, never hopeful

Say only what is true and built. No forecasts Lingua can’t keep, no scores that look precise and aren’t, no features promised before they exist. When Lingua doesn’t know, it says so plainly, or says nothing at all.

Taken out2026-10-26: the first start’s “B1 in about 7 months” and its pace verdicts. Not accurate enough to say.

V.The learner holds the pen

Whatever Lingua decides for you can be undone: a word marked known can be unmarked, a level changed, a card edited. Nothing large happens behind the learner’s back. Saving a word is not the same as scheduling it, and one click never quietly grows a deck of fifty.

BorrowedFrom the Lingyu blueprint (2026-10-25): “saving is not scheduling”; anything generated is editable and can be dismissed.

VI.Wide and calm

Use the width: columns, side panels, sticky rails, a list beside its detail instead of a page after it. Every page fits a 1366 × 768 laptop with no little scroll bar sneaking in. The reader and long lists are the only things that scroll, and they do it on purpose.

SizesCheck at 1280, 1440 and 1366 × 768. Shortcuts: Alt 1–8 for the rooms; the rest in the Shortcuts dialog.

If you only remember one: the language first, everything else quiet.

Chapter 3

The voice

Lingua talks like a friend who reads a lot: informal, warm, a little dry. It likes to make you smile, but never at the cost of being clear.

One smile per page. A funny subtitle is a gift; three of them are a stand-up set. The joke goes where nobody has to read it to act: a subtitle, a quiet line under a field. Never inside an instruction.

The user’s outlineFor the first start: “minimal, informal, bookish, building up the excitement, with funny subtitles” (5.1-2b).

Titles ask or name. “What language will it be?” “So, how far along are you?” “Choose your study light.” Short enough to take in at a glance, and a real question when there is one.

Buttons say what happens. “Continue with Deutsch” says more than “Next”. One main button per page.

Plain where it matters. Money, privacy, errors and deleting get the plainest words in the app. “Could not save your choices. Please try again.”

Real linesFrom Onboarding.tsx: the error above, and the welcome toast, “Welcome to Lingua, {name}. Happy reading!”

Small manners. British spelling, as in colour and favourite. Curly quotes, and the language’s own quotation marks when its touches are on. No exclamation marks in the interface; the welcome toast may keep its one.

The accent is a bookmark, not a highlighter. One terracotta thing per area: the chosen language, the next step, the new word. If two things on one card are terracotta, one of them is wrong.

Chapter 6

Space, corners and light

A Lingua page breathes like a well-set book: generous margins, one clear column, and things grouped by space before they are grouped by boxes.

Lines before shadows. A hairline separates; a shadow lifts. Most things only need the line. When something does float, one shadow is enough, and it is warm, never grey.

Lightelev-1 a card at rest · elev-2 something floating: the map, a popover · elev-3 dialogs and drawers. All tinted espresso.

Round, not bubbly. Pills for small choices, generous corners for cards and dialogs, gentler ones inside them. An inner corner is always smaller than the one around it.

TailwindButtons rounded-lg/xl/2xl by size; cards and dialogs rounded-3xl; empty states rounded-2xl.

One screen. A desktop page fits 1366 × 768 without a scroll bar. If it doesn’t fit, take something away before making something smaller.

Since2026-10-25, 5c044f9: desktop screens on one screen, no tiny scroll.

Columns over stacks. A sticky rail for filters, a panel beside a list, two cards side by side that end on the same line.

Chapter 7

Motion

Things in Lingua settle like paper: they move a little, then they are still. Motion says where something came from. It never performs.

Only transform and opacity move. Never a width, a height or a top: the reader has to stay at sixty frames a second.

Wheresrc/design/motion.ts; the rules in docs/PERFORMANCE.md.

The first start turns its pages, 48 pixels sideways with a six-degree tilt. Elsewhere, pages rise 8 pixels as they appear.

Reduced motion means none, not less.

Nothing is slower than the learner. If they click again before something has finished moving, the second click wins.

Chapter 8

Drawings, icons and flags

Drawings are one fine line in the colour of soft ink, with one small thing in terracotta: a bookmark, a tick, a wisp of sound. They are small, and they never explain what the words already say.

The lineStroke 1.3, round caps and joins, currentColor in ink-soft. See the open book and the three vignettes in IntroPages.tsx.

Icons are Lucide, as lines. A room keeps its icon wherever it appears: the sidebar, Home, a link from another room.

This bookThe little room drawings here are for the book only. In the app, the rooms use their Lucide icons.

Flags are drawn, all the same size and corner. Where one country’s flag would be wrong for a language, it gets its own emblem (the Senyera for Catalan, the Ikurriña for Basque, the Frisian flag) or a badge with its script on soft paper: ਪੰ for Punjabi, Š / Ћ for Serbo-Croatian, सं for Sanskrit, ᚱ for Old Norse.

Wheresrc/design/flags.tsx. Script badges set the glyph in the reading font on a paper square, so they sit calmly beside real flags.

An empty page may have a drawing. A full page doesn’t need one.

A touch of the language

When you learn French, Lingua looks a little French. Each core language lends the app a heading face, one ornament and a lean of the accent. It is a touch, not a costume: the reading text and the learner’s palette never change.

Part Three

Page by page

Chapter 9

The anatomy of a page

Most Lingua pages are made of the same five parts. Get these right and the page is nearly done.

  1. The room, lit in the sidebar. Nothing else tells you where you are.
  2. A title and one line. The title in Literata; the line under it in the helper’s voice, and that’s where a smile goes.
  3. The one thing to do, in the accent fill, top right or under the work. One per page.
  4. The work. The language, a list, a player: the biggest, calmest thing.
  5. What’s beside it, and the quiet edges: a panel or rail; counts, More, settings.
Use these firstButton, Card, Dialog, Drawer, EmptyState, Popover, SearchInput, Segmented, Select, Slider, Switch, Tooltip: src/design/primitives.
Chapter 10 · Lingua 6 begins here

The first start

The first start is the first page of the book. It should feel like opening one: unhurried, a little exciting, and over before it gets long.

The planLingua 6-1 in docs/LINGUA-6-PLAN.md, from the user’s notes of 2026-10-26. Code: src/app/onboarding.
Your name·Welcome·What Lingua can do·Language·Level·You speak·Why·Goal·Study light·Plans

Three pages to say hello, then four steps to set up, and the plans. “You speak” and “Why” were taken out: the fewer questions before the first page of reading, the better.

DotsOnly the steps get dots, not the hello pages. Every page fits 1366 × 768, except the language page, where the list peeks in on purpose.

Your name

“First things first.” One field for a first name, and Continue waits until there is one. No skip. The quiet line stays: “It stays on this device. We just like to be polite.”

Out“Skip for now”, and the Lingua mark above the title: the header already has the wordmark.

The language

The world first, wide. Click a country, and a panel opens with the country itself as its title, its flag and its name, set properly, then the languages you can learn there. No tabs. Under the map, the six core languages as small book covers, each wearing a touch of its language; under them every other language, larger than before, by tier. Each tier says plainly what it has and what it doesn’t have yet.

TiersCore · Full · Beta · Basics · Experimental. One table, TIERS in src/core/lang/languages.ts, with has and not yet.

The level

Seven steps, A0 to C2, like a staircase. Hover one or choose it, and a small card pops up with what that level could mean: what you can read, follow and say there, and what Lingua will do about it. No example sentences, no test.

The cardSprings in on snappy; arrow keys move along the steps. From A2 up it says the most common words start as known.

The goal

A level to aim for, and minutes a day. Nothing forecasts. Instead each choice says what it means: the goal in the level card’s words, the minutes in what they hold in Lingua: “Cards, a few pages and a song.”

Out“B1 in about 7 months”, “Ambitious. We like it.” Not accurate enough.

Study light, and the plans

Every palette as a small page of the app, with Follow my device first. Then the plans, which promise only what is true, with Continue free as clear as the rest.

Plansfeatures/plans/PlanPitch. Max shows monthly first.

Ask yourself

  1. Can someone reach the map in under a minute?
  2. Does any page promise something Lingua can’t measure?
  3. Is there more than one joke on a page, or one inside an instruction?

Chapter 19

Every page’s states

A page is not done when it looks right full. It is done when it also looks right empty, slow, wrong and offline.

The first timeSay why it’s empty, and give one way in.
LoadingKeep the page’s shape. If it takes more than a moment, say what’s happening.
Nothing foundShow what was searched for, and a way to clear it.
Went wrongKeep what the learner typed, say what failed, and offer to try again.
OfflineSay what still works. Most of Lingua does.
Not in this planSay what it is and what it costs, and that saying no loses nothing.
ComingDimmed, with a word on hover. Never a button that does nothing.
A lotFive hundred books, ten thousand words: does it still breathe?

A toast is a whisper, not a record. Anything the learner must know stays on the page after the toast has gone.

PrimitivesEmptyState for the first time and nothing found; Toaster for whispers.

KeysStay on the device, travel only in request headers, never in a URL.
